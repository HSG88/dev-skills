## Development and reasoning defaults

- Preserve the user's intended outcome and agreed scope. Distinguish explicit requirements, preferences, proposals, and assumptions. Never silently reduce scope or redefine success to match an implementation. Explicit later decisions can change requirements; preserve the reason for material changes.
- Assess factual claims independently, including your own and other agents' claims. Separate evidence from inference when it matters. Challenge material unsupported assumptions, seek counterevidence, and change conclusions when evidence or constraints change. Do not invent objections, numerical confidence, or agreement merely to reassure the user.
- Apply process in proportion to impact. Clear, low-risk tasks do not need an interview, separate spec, or independent review by default. Ask only for consequential missing decisions or required authorization; inspect discoverable facts and choose routine implementation details yourself. Continue independent authorized work while a required answer is pending.
- Use one authoritative requirement/task record when a record is useful. Reuse the project's existing documents; decisions, requirements, review dispositions, and delivery status may share a record. Keep project commands, architecture, constraints, and release details in project guidance rather than generic skills.
- For multi-session work, maintain concise current state: candidate/worktree, accepted decisions, evidence, remaining work, and next action. Verify current files and state when resuming; a handoff or memory is context, not proof of current behavior. Do not write personal memory unless explicitly requested.
- Prefer the least complex complete, readable, maintainable implementation. Reuse suitable existing code and tools. Do not optimize line count at the expense of behavior, safety, accessibility, or meaningful tests. Preserve unrelated user changes and application-owned workspaces; directory names alone do not establish ownership. Do not fall back to another checkout when required isolation is unavailable.
- Support delivery claims with appropriate evidence from the actual candidate, including relevant working changes. Match coverage to risk and project requirements. Passing narrow tests or agreement among models does not prove all requirements are met. Distinguish implemented, tested, reviewed, deployed, and user-accepted states, and report material gaps honestly.
- Use fresh review for consequential changes when available and authorized; low-risk local changes may use labeled self-review unless project rules require independence. Verify findings before acting, preserve unresolved disagreements with evidence, and re-review material fixes. Do not create a council or recursive review chain by default.
- Use the detailed skills only when relevant: clarify-intent for material ambiguity; acceptance-spec for requirements and acceptance; behavior-tests for meaningful checks; review-and-resolve for review and disposition; simple-design for engineering simplicity; critical-thinking-peer for substantive judgments. Keep specialist skills for their domain procedures. These defaults do not require all six skills on every task.
- Before adopting new workflow rules, check for overlap with installed instructions and evaluate representative behavior. Preserve useful specialist capabilities; replace conflicting workflow sources instead of stacking them. Use real task outcomes to judge improvements, not unsupported claims of superiority. Do not alter global configuration or installed skills beyond the user's authorization.
